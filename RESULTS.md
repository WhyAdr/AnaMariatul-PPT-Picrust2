SC ITS reanalysis completed — 23 October 2026, Asia/Jakarta.

The requested environments, primer investigation, clustering trials, fungal ecological annotation, EPI2ME workflow, downstream postprocessing, and exploratory fungal PICRUSt2 run are complete. The primary deliverable is a sequence-based, consensus-supported OTU dataset. Original provider files retain their original checksums. All required final validations passed.

I selected the stricter amplicon_sorter workflow for the requested emphasis on a reliable community/guild overview. It yields **41 supported 98% ITS OTUs**, of which **34 enter the conservative fungal dataset**. These are operational sequence groups, not 41 verified species or exact ASVs. VSEARCH alternatives and the complete comparison evidence remain available.

The sample remains unreplicated and lacks available controls. Habitat is unconfirmed; the workbook suggests a possible Sonneratia/mangrove association. Results describe the delivered sample under stated processing choices and cannot support treatment comparisons, environmental associations, or estimates of organism activity.

**Primer investigation and preprocessing.** The classic White ITS1 and ITS4 sequences were investigated in both orientations: ITS1 `TCCGTAGGTGAACCTGCGG`; ITS4 `TCCTCCGCTTATTGATATGC`. Both the delivered raw and provider-filtered reads retain substantial primer sequence. Full-length primer matches allowing up to 15% edits in terminal 100 bp windows identify a canonical pair in 59,918 raw reads and 42,211 provider-filtered reads. The sequences agree with the [UNITE primer reference](https://unite.ut.ee/primers.php).

The primary subset requires Q≥12, the canonical terminal pair, no exact internal full-primer signal, linked Cutadapt trimming, at least 200 bp after trimming, and at most 1% N. Of the paired reads, 2,528 were excluded for internal primer signal; an additional 4,951 failed Q12; 35 candidate reads failed the final Cutadapt gates. The final subset contains 52,404 reads, including **13,021 reads originally longer than 900 bp**. Consequently, the provider’s 900 bp maximum was not carried forward. Internal primer signals can indicate tandem or other products and are retained in the audit; their absence is not a complete chimera test. This conservative primer-based selection excludes 50,611 delivered reads and should not be mistaken for an unbiased census of all input molecules.

**Clustering comparison.** A fixed read-ID hash split reserved 10,661 reads for validation, with 41,743 training reads used for discovery and polishing. Pilots used 6,000 training and 2,000 held-out reads. Raw-read VSEARCH pilots at 90%, 95%, and 97% identity generated 318, 1,734, and 3,501 clusters; the increase at strict thresholds indicates substantial fragmentation in noisy individual reads. The amplicon_sorter default and stricter pilot produced 16 and 27 consensus groups, respectively. The stricter pilot assigned 90.75% of held-out reads uniquely, with 1% ambiguous.

Full trials used VSEARCH at 90% and 95% and amplicon_sorter with group similarity 94%, read addition 90%, and consensus merging 98%. Supported amplicon consensuses were polished twice with training-only reads using Racon. ITSx located marker regions and preserved boundary/origin flags. Equivalent normalized ITS regions were grouped at 98% identity with 95% bidirectional coverage. Counts were assigned against the full amplicon representatives and then collapsed through that ITS map: trimming the reference boundaries therefore does not manufacture read-coverage losses or duplicate counts. Core support requires at least 10 uniquely assigned reads and at least 3 independent held-out reads.

The primary sorter workflow uses 46 initial consensus groups and retains 41 supported ITS OTUs after normalization/refinement. It assigns 9,934 of 10,661 held-out reads, with one ambiguous. Technical partition agreement with VSEARCH 95% is high on 45,353 shared core-assigned reads (adjusted Rand index 0.98485); VSEARCH 90% agreement is 0.99856 on 39,117 shared reads. This supports consistency of the abundant sequence groups, not proof of species boundaries or exclusion of overmerging. Stricter species-level work would need additional validation. Fixed parent seeds and the hash split are recorded; multiprocessing scheduling can still affect exact reruns of the upstream sorter.

**Chimera and origin checks.** VSEARCH de novo and UNITE-reference UCHIME screens did not flag any supported primary core OTU as chimeric. This is an algorithmic result, not proof that the original sample is chimera-free. Seven core OTUs have non-fungal or ambiguous ITSx origin evidence and were excluded from the fungal prediction input. Two have full-query UNITE matches within Nematoda (including a Cervidellus match); the remaining preliminary origin labels are kept as uncertain. The reference screen returned a borderline `?` for OTU_9 (2,101 reads), which the original Y-only exclusion rule retained. Excluding this OTU reduces annotated coverage to 365 FungalTraits reads (0.70% of preprocessed reads) and 3,493 probable/highly probable FUNGuild reads (6.67%). Most primary OTUs have partial ITSx boundary evidence (37 of 41); these should not all be described as verified complete ITS sequences. These qualifications do not change the preserved analysis inputs or outputs. Universal ITS amplification and a fungi-only classifier should not be treated as proof that every sequenced molecule is fungal.

**Taxonomy and ecological annotation.** Representative sequences were searched against the official UNITE all-eukaryote dynamic release, DOI [10.15156/BIO/3301243](https://doi.org/10.15156/BIO/3301243). Taxonomy requires at least 90% query coverage, 80% reference coverage and 90% identity; near-best hits within 2% of the best bit score are reconciled conservatively. Species and genus labels have additional provisional identity caps. These are practical safeguards, not universal fungal taxonomic thresholds. Placeholder/incertae-sedis names are not treated as known genera. Several abundant OTUs match environmental references well but remain unnamed above genus/species, which limits guild coverage.

FungalTraits 1.2 was matched using genus plus phylum and retains primary/secondary lifestyles. FUNGuild v1.1 was run against a byte-pinned 13,017-record microeco-distributed snapshot because its live service failed. The execution wrapper supplies the local snapshot and fixes inconsistent species-token normalization; original source and the compatibility change are preserved. FUNGuild probable/highly probable assignments form the main guild summary, while possible and unmapped entries remain visible. Some FUNGuild matches are at family rank, so database coverage figures are not directly equivalent measures of accuracy. Mixed guild labels remain combined categories rather than being counted multiple times. See the [FungalTraits paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13225-020-00466-2) and [FUNGuild documentation](https://github.com/UMNFuN/FUNGuild).

The low annotated fraction is a substantive result. Guild labels such as plant pathogen or saprotroph describe literature-derived capacities associated with the matched taxon; they do not establish disease, decomposition rate, or activity here. Unassigned reads are not evidence of absent ecological functions. Across the VSEARCH alternatives, mapped read totals were similar (FungalTraits 2,554–2,560; probable FUNGuild 5,612–5,638), despite differences in the low-support cluster tail.

**EPI2ME and the linked postprocessor.** EPI2ME wf-16s v1.6.1 ran successfully with the official ITS-containing NCBI reference, minimap2, species output, Q12, length 200–2000, 95% identity and 90% reference coverage. Its genuine params, assignments, version record, abundance TSV and HTML report are preserved. Of 52,404 input reads, fastcat retained 52,361; 43 additional reads failed Q12 after primer trimming. Of those retained, 608 reads passed classification and 51,753 were unclassified. Many raw alignments cover short conserved portions of a reference; this explains much of the contrast with provider Centrifuge assignments. The classification gates were not relaxed to reproduce the earlier high yield.

The [ont-wf16S-amplicon-postprocess stack](https://github.com/WhyAdr/ont-wf16S-amplicon-postprocess) completed against those genuine outputs using R 4.5.3 and its synchronized package lock. QC, alpha diversity, composition and Kraken/Krona/Pavian exports completed. Beta diversity, ordination and shared-group analyses skipped appropriately for one sample. FAPROTAX was not requested because it does not provide fungal functional inference. The frozen local taxonomy cache leaves three unresolved nodes, explicitly recorded. These outputs summarize the 608-read classifier-passing subset and should not be interpreted as the primary full community profile. All 73 manifest-listed artifacts were independently hash-verified.

**PICRUSt2 outcome.** The matching fungal FASTA and integer-count BIOM passed format and identifier validation. PICRUSt2 2.6.3 completed with its fungal single-reference pipeline, fungal ITS reference, ITS copy-number traits, fungal EC traits, fungal pathway map, minimum aligned fraction 0.5 and default maximum NSTI 2.0. Five OTUs representing 10,935 reads failed the alignment gate. Two more, representing 842 reads, exceeded the NSTI cutoff. Final predictions represent 27 OTUs and 28,587 reads: **54.55% of the preprocessed subset, or 27.75% of delivered raw reads**. Output contains 840 positive EC categories and 67 predicted pathways. Weighted NSTI is **1.047** (median eligible OTU NSTI 0.913).

The substantial reference distances and incomplete representation limit interpretation. The [developers’ fungal guidance](https://github.com/picrust/picrust2/wiki/Frequently-Asked-Questions) reports weak fungal predictive performance and discourages routine use. These files are an exploratory hypothesis-generating supplement, not measurements of genes, expression, pathway activity, or nutrient cycling. Ecological conclusions should rely primarily on supported sequence groups, explicit taxonomy uncertainty and cautious trait assignments.

**Deliverables and reproducibility.** Use `conda activate ONT-otu` for preprocessing; `amplicon_sorter --version` reports the pinned 2025-10-09 source. Use `conda activate ONT-ecoguild` for annotation; `funguild-offline INPUT.tsv` uses its installed recorded snapshot. FungalTraits data are installed under that environment’s `share/sc-its-tools/references/`. Conda exports, source snapshots, scripts, intermediate assignments, rejected-read categories and final job statuses are retained. The existing ITSx and PICRUSt2 environments were used without installing into them. CPU-only jobs had explicit limits and detached host sessions; no cron job, service or recurring automation was installed.
